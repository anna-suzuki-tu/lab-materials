LAB TOOLS — PRESENTATION PEER REVIEW

Presentation Peer Review

Training the ability to watch a talk critically — Both the audience and the presenter are trained here
The audience trains critical listening; the presenter gets concrete points to improve. 聴衆は「発表を批評的に見る力」を養い、発表者は具体的な改善点を得る。

Purpose

When we rehearse in the lab before a conference or a review, every member of the audience fills in this sheet.

  • For the audience: practice in not letting someone else's talk end at "easy / hard to understand," but putting into words where and what did not get across
  • For the presenter: an opportunity to receive concrete points for improvement from several perspectives

Principles

  • Write honestly, without deferring to seniority. "I did not understand" is also important feedback
  • Do not reject the presenter; connect it to the presenter's improvement
  • The presenter should not feel rejected, but use it to improve
  • B4 students may write "the parts I did not understand" just as they are. That in itself is an important comment

Structure of the sheet

Section 0 — Before listening
What do you most want to know from this talk (one sentence)? Writing it before listening keeps you from being passive.

Section 1 — The flow of the story
Summarize, in your own words and in one or two lines each, the six elements: Background (what is not known) / Purpose (why this research) / Methods (what was done and how) / Results (what was found) / Interpretation (what it means) / Conclusion (what the claim is). Write "unclear" for any part you could not follow. That becomes the most useful feedback.

Section 2 — Readability of the slides (1–5 points)

  • Does each slide have one message?
  • Can the claim be understood from the title alone (Title = assertion)?
  • Can the figures and graphs be understood without text?

Add slide numbers or concrete examples.

Section 3 — What hit hardest, and the biggest question
The slide that left the strongest impression (and why it worked), and the point that most made you ask "why?". Be as specific as possible.

Section 4 — From the viewpoint of a conference audience
What would a specialist "want to know more about"? Where would a non-specialist need a little more explanation? Think from both viewpoints.

Section 5 — What to apply to your own talk and research
Treating this talk as a learning opportunity, one thing you want to try, or avoid, in your own talks and research.

Workflow

  1. The presenter creates one page on the Presentation Peer Review board in Notion (presenter, conference name, date)
  2. The audience fills in Section 0 before the talk, Sections 1–4 during and right after the talk, and Section 5 after the discussion
  3. The presenter reads the comments and uses them to improve. No rebuttal is needed
