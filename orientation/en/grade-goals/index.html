Grade-Specific Goals — The more freedom, the more responsibility

The more freedom, the more responsibility

As you go B4 → master's → PhD, your freedom grows: choice of theme, use of time, how you run your research. And as freedom grows, so does the responsibility you take on — Suzuki Lab's view of academic years is a "two-axis model of responsibility." The horizontal axis is research responsibility; the vertical axis is community responsibility. Moving up a year means moving toward the upper right of this plane.

Responsibility by year

  • 🚀 B4: experience and integrity
    Run the research cycle once. Share results and struggles honestly. Perfection is not required. Running the cycle is the responsibility.
  • 🏁 Master's: completion and supporting juniors
    Submit one Original. Support and guide the B4s. The responsibility to complete.
  • 🏆 PhD: defining an axis, and culture
    Define your own research axis. Protect and grow the lab's culture. A Review is one form of establishing an axis.

Expectations are explicit

This is not optional. It is the baseline.

Quarter Plan: not a progress report, but a visualization of your thinking

Strategy is designed in quarters (90 days). Divide the year's research journey (from theme decision to paper submission) into Q1–Q4, and review the structure each quarter — the Quarter Plan is not a progress-report document but a tool for visualizing the structure of your thinking.

Why 90 days: research is a long-distance run, but design should be done over short distances. In a short stretch, you notice structural drift right away. The B4 goal is not perfection but "the experience of one cycle" — 90-day design supports that.

And before moving, identify where you are (Identity Snapshot — YOU ARE HERE). With a vague current position, you cannot design forward motion.

→ How to use the Quarter Plan, the template, and the roadmaps by year are explained in Lab Tools "Quarter Plan".

The B4 year: counting back from deadlines

B4 is not a year in which you only do research. External events — entrance exams, job hunting, coursework — press on your research. So design by counting back from the deadlines.

QuarterGoalNote
Q1 (Apr–Jun)Fix the themeRuns alongside the curriculum (courses)
Q2 (Jul–Sep)FramingThe period when entrance exams and job hunting press on research
Q3 (Oct–Dec)FiguresFinalize the figures
Q4 (Jan–Feb)Thesis write-upThesis submission — one full cycle of a year's research

The four typical collapse patterns: ① stopping for entrance exams ② going silent when stuck ③ not sharing while waiting for completion ④ collapsing under doing everything at once. The Quarter Plan is the device that prevents these.

Note that what is evaluated is not "results." Understanding, logical presentation, Q&A, the structure of your materials — all by-products of structured thinking. Complete one cycle, and the skills follow.

The master's two years are "one long cycle" — it is decided in M1

  • M1: exploration and design — Focus on Framing. By mid-M1, "Axis fixed" — fix the axis
  • M2: convergence and completion — The acceleration phase. Those who panic in M2 did not design in M1

The reality of M2: first draft in September, mid-term presentation in November, submission deadline in January. Time vanishes suddenly. That is why you design in M1.

The structure of the PhD's three years: build, expand, integrate

  1. D1: build the axis — Axis Construction. Review paper (externalizing the hypothesis)
  2. D2: expand — Original Expansion. Deepening methods, independence
  3. D3: integrate — Integration & Positioning. Designing the structure of the thesis

"Axis-less PhDs will drift."

Chapter 08 summary: Freedom and responsibility are proportional ／ Baselines: B4 = one full cycle, M = one Original, PhD = axis + culture ／ Design in 90-day units (Quarter Plan) ／ Count back from external events ／ It is decided by the design in M1 and D1.
