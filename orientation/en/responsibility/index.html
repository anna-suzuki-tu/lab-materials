Responsibility

Responsibility — Taking responsibility for a belief means deciding who answers, for what, and how far

A paper is not an achievement. It is a device for accountability

In Chapter 01 we said that a paper is "the medium through which you take on accountability for your own belief." This chapter divides that responsibility into four: who bears it (authorship), whether you are honest with the data (integrity), whether others can verify it (reproducibility), and how you treat others' work (attribution and citation).

A thesis is internal responsibility; a journal is external verification. Writing a thesis alone leaves the inquiry inside the lab. Inquiry is completed in the public space (Lab → Scientific Community), and the claims that go out into that space carry the responsibilities described below.

1｜Authorship: who is responsible for which claim

Being an author does not mean having your name listed. It means standing in the position of answering, when questioned, for the paper's claims.

  • The first author is the main person responsible for the study (experiments, analysis, writing), and bears responsibility for the claim as a whole
  • A co-author is someone who made an important contribution to carrying it out (providing data, joint experiments, key analytical support), and bears responsibility for the parts they were involved in
  • The corresponding author (usually the supervisor) answers inquiries and bears responsibility for the coherence of the whole paper

Providing data only, or receiving supervision only, belongs in the acknowledgments, not the author list. Conversely, once your name is on the author list, "that part wasn't mine" does not hold. Author order is based on contribution: tentatively set before writing, re-evaluated before completion, and fixed with everyone's agreement before submission. Procedural details are in Lab Forum Lectures "Writing Papers".

2｜Integrity: honest with the data, with people, with yourself

Falsification, fabrication, and plagiarism are the three cardinal violations of research ethics, and there is no room for debate. But the question of responsibility starts much earlier.

  • Do not hide results that did not work or differed from expectation. If you exclude inconvenient data, decide the exclusion criteria first and state them
  • "Silence when stuck" is the opposite of integrity. Share stagnation too (Lab Values 02)
  • Do not overstate the level of the claim (Chapter 02). If you can show only correlation, write "description." Do not write a regularity found by induction as if it had been generalized deductively

Integrity is a matter of ethics and, at the same time, of the strength of the paper. A paper that writes its limitations honestly withstands review better.

3｜Reproducibility: leave it in a form others can verify

A claim that has gone out into the public space is meaningless unless others can question it. Being able to question means being able to follow the same steps.

  • Record the date, conditions, parameters, and results in the Research Log (Chapter 10; Lab Tools "Research Log & Thinking Log")
  • Code and experimental conditions go to GitHub. Save data according to the naming rules, and leave everything behind when you graduate (for where everything lives, see Lab Tools "Lab Infrastructure")
  • Write the paper's Methods at a granularity readers can reproduce. "Details omitted" is the abandonment of reproducibility

Reproducibility is for others and, at the same time, for your future self. Six months from now, you are another person.

4｜Attribution and citation: how to treat others' work

When you use others' data, ideas, or figures, always cite the source. Citation is an obligation and, at the same time, proof of your own positioning (Chapter 03, Step 1). Use copyright-free figures, or cite the source. Text and figures made with generative AI are also subject to disclosure of how and to what extent they were used.

"Taking responsibility," as a way of speaking

Responsibility is not a burden; it is the other side of holding a belief. Say "this is what I think," and answer when questioned. Becoming a researcher means standing in that position. Even as an undergraduate, that position begins the moment you write your name on your thesis.

Chapter 06 summary: A paper is a device for accountability ／ An author is someone who answers for the claim ／ Integrity is ethics and the strength of the paper ／ Reproducibility is for others and for your future self ／ Citation is an obligation and proof of positioning
