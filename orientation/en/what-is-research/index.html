What is Research?

Inquiry, not answer collection
Part I Principles begins here. This part is about research in general. It applies to any field. 01 What is Research? ／ 02 Inquiry Process and the Framework of Science

Research is inquiry

What we call research is, at heart, inquiry. It is not a job of piling up answers toward a "final answer," but the practice of raising a question, placing a hypothesis, testing it, and updating belief — again and again. This chapter explains how that inquiry is structured, and what role a paper plays within it.

Inquiry is the process that turns "doubt" into "belief"

The starting point of inquiry is doubt — the sense that "something is off." Keep turning the cycle Question → Hypothesis → Test (the Inquiry Cycle), and doubt eventually becomes belief. But this belief is not "the right answer." It is probabilistic, provisional, and updatable.

The Inquiry Cycle as a whole:

  1. Notice a misalignment — a sense that something is off
  2. Question — raise a question
  3. Hypothesis — propose a hypothesis
  4. Test — verify (go back and forth as many times as needed)
  5. Belief — arrive at a provisional belief, then on to the next misalignment ↺

The belief you obtain is a Provisional Belief — always open to updating. Updating is the essence of inquiry (Theory ≠ Observation).

Inquiry starts from misalignment — misalignment generates questions

Where do questions come from? From two kinds of misalignment.

  • Observation ≠ Understanding — the gap between what you observed and what you understand
  • Ideal ≠ Reality — the gap between how things should be and how they are

GAP → Inquiry starts here. Misalignment is not a sign of failure — it is the starting point of inquiry.

Belief is not "correctness." It is probability

Understand precisely the nature of the "belief" that inquiry produces.

  1. Always provisional — nothing more than the best estimate at this moment
  2. Always updatable — rewritten by new evidence
  3. Its strength varies with evidence — a probability distribution over hypothesis space

What to seek is not "absolute correctness" but the most probable belief — keep holding the best estimate at this moment.

Cognition has the same structure — the brain, too, updates itself through error

This structure is, in fact, how our brain works. The brain predicts the world, finds the error between prediction and observation, and updates itself — cognition and inquiry share one structure.

In the intuitive version of the free energy principle (K. Friston): the brain tries to minimize prediction error (misalignment). Large error produces discomfort, which triggers action or updating. Small error settles into stable belief. Beyond updating lies a refined belief. The "discomfort of misalignment" you feel in research is a sign that the brain is asking to update.

A paper is a medium that fixes questions and beliefs

So what is a paper? Not "the announcement of a final answer." It is a snapshot of the best belief at this moment. In the flow Doubt → Inquiry → Paper (Fixed Belief), a paper is the device that "fixes," once, a belief that keeps moving.

What happens once it is fixed:

  1. Paper — fix the belief. "This is our current best belief."
  2. Critique — being fixed makes it open to criticism. Peer review and verification become possible
  3. Next Question — critique generates the next question, and the next inquiry cycle opens

That is why we publish. A paper is not an achievement but a medium for receiving "error" — the act of submitting a probabilistic belief to the public space in order to receive critique and feedback from the world. Research does not end. It keeps updating. That is our responsibility as inquirers.

Research is never finished. It keeps updating, leaving a sharper question

Belief is provisional, and a paper generates the next question. So the goal of research is not a "final answer" but arriving at a more refined, better-framed question. Answers are by-products obtained along the way, and good research always leaves behind "a question sharper than before."

It is not a job with a deadline that gets "finished." It is a perpetual inquiry cycle with no linear goal. The next chapter deals with how to place a hypothesis within this cycle — the types and stances of reasoning.

Chapter 01 summary: Research = inquiry ／ Inquiry is a cycle that turns doubt into belief, and it starts from misalignment ／ Belief is probabilistic, provisional, updatable ／ A paper fixes a belief so that it can receive critique ／ So research is never finished — it continues, leaving a sharper question.
